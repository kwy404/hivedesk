How it works

One City.
Every machine you trust.

Your PC is the first building. Friends bring theirs. Agents live in the rooms, take the tasks and think on whichever machine the scheduler picks, always within what the owner allowed.

  1. 1

    Create your City

    Start hivedesk. Your PC becomes a building with rooms where agents work: Design, Code, Review, Test, Ops and Library.

  2. 2

    Invite a friend

    Share six words. You both compare check words, you choose what they may do, and their machine rises next to yours.

  3. 3

    Agents take the tasks

    An Architect designs, a Developer writes, QA runs the tests and a Reviewer reads the diff. A failure loops back to the author, at most 3 times.

  4. 4

    Borrow a GPU

    When a model does not fit on your machine, the scheduler asks a friend's GPU for a lease, says why, and stops the moment either of you says so.

The City

The City is the system.
Nothing on it is decoration.

Buildings are machines, rooms are places with their own tools, and every task walks through the rooms on its way to done. Every number is measured, or the panel says it is not measured.

Rooms with rules. While an agent works in a room, its tools are cut to that room's. The task board shows every task, its state and the loop it is on.

Every room says what it allows. The panel lists the tools allowed inside, who is there now and the tasks in the room.

The same events, indoors. The Office shows the crew at work, the current job and a count of finished tasks.

Every number has a source. Live samples say when they were taken, and what was not measured says not measured.

A real workbench. Explorer, tabs, format and save, and the commands the crew runs in the run log.

Nothing is allowed until it is ticked. The computer being reached always decides, and a red bar with Stop stays on top while someone is connected.

    The workbench

    A real code workbench.
    Files, search, terminal and git.

    Open the folders you choose, next to the crew's workspace. Each folder is a grant you can remove, and the crew writes only where you allow it.

    Explorer and editors

    Create, rename, move and delete. Tabs in up to three split groups, diagnostics, go to definition and symbols for JavaScript and TypeScript. Deleting a folder asks you to confirm how many files it holds.

    Search and replace

    Across every file, with regular expressions, case, whole word and include or exclude patterns. Replace shows a diff per file first, and skips a file that changed since.

    Terminal

    A real terminal on Windows and macOS. On Linux it needs a build of its native part, otherwise a basic terminal over pipes, labeled as such. Agents never get a terminal.

    Source control

    Status, diff, stage, unstage, discard, commit, branches, checkout, stashes and the log, in one panel.

    Share a terminal

    Let a friend watch or type for 5, 15 or 60 minutes. The tab shows a shared badge and a kill button, and the audit log counts the bytes, never the text.

    Code together

    Shared documents with both cursors over the same encrypted session. A friend's edits wait for your approval, new files included.

    Remote screen

    See a friend's screen.
    Control only when they tick it.

    A 10 digit address finds a machine, but it is not a password: the owner sees who is calling, with their check words, and every box starts unticked.

    For5 min15 min60 min

    RefuseAccept and choose a screen

    • Off until you allow it. A machine answers its address only while "Let partners reach this computer" is on, and that is off by default.
    • Every grant expires. 5, 15 or 60 minutes, view only unless control is ticked, with a 60 second countdown on the question.
    • A kill switch. A banner with Stop while shared, and Ctrl Alt Shift K ends everything on Windows.
    • No input on a locked screen. While the screen is locked, the viewer sees a card and every key is dropped.
    • Several screens, quality presets and chat. Clipboard text and images, and files up to 2 GB that resume where they stopped.
    • Unattended access, if you set it. For known peers with a password; 5 wrong tries lock it for 15 minutes.

    From your phone, too. Pair a phone with the QR code in the hivedesk window, then approve, chat and follow jobs from it. Each phone has its own grants.

    Where control works in 1.0: Windows, and Linux on X11 with xdotool and loginctl installed. macOS is view only, because control needs a signed app and the downloads are not signed yet. Wayland is view only too.

    Models

    The right model for each machine.
    And a meter for the giant ones.

    A catalog of 57 open source models from 0.5B to 72B parameters, for coding, reasoning and chat, each in quantized files from 2 to 8 bits. hivedesk recommends one for your hardware and asks before any download, with its size, license and source.

    Pool a giant model with friends

    A 70B model at 4 bits needs about 45 GB with its context. No single consumer PC has that, but a City might.

    Experimental, off by default

    The meter is real math in the app: GPU memory and RAM counted apart, the speed of CPU memory and one network round trip per remote machine per token, and it unlocks only at 1 token per second or more. Pooling is in 1.0 as an experimental switch, off by default: one model runs across the machines of friends who granted it, over the encrypted p2p link, and its speed depends on the network link between them.

    Security

    Default deny.
    Every power is named, granted, timed and logged.

    The engine of the machine that owns a resource decides, not a browser tab. A guest cannot pick your model, open a shell or see a file you did not share.

    An identity per install

    An Ed25519 key, shown to people as six fingerprint words. A key that cannot be read is an error, never a silent new identity.

    Invites that burn

    Six words, 66 bits, one use, 10 minutes, burned by the first handshake that starts. Then check words on both screens and your Accept.

    Grants, off by default

    When a friend joins, only See the City is ticked. GPU, CPU, RAM, storage, terminal, screen, clipboard and files are separate grants with limits; screen and terminal grants end within 60 minutes.

    Leases with deadlines

    A lent job holds a 15 second lease renewed by heartbeats, a hard deadline and a token limit. It runs only on the lender's local model, never on their cloud key.

    A hash chained audit log

    Joins, grants, revokes, jobs and remote sessions, month by month, each line chained to the last. It never stores words, keys, prompts or answers.

    Encrypted end to end

    X25519 and HKDF for the keys, ChaCha20-Poly1305 for every frame, rekeyed every 60 minutes. Relays only ever carry ciphertext.

    Answers from another machine are untrusted. Any tool call that comes from a turn a friend's GPU served needs your approval in every approval mode, new files included.

    Honest about the limits

    What 1.0 does not do.
    Yet, or on purpose.

    Each item is marked in the app too. Nothing here is faked to look finished.

    Peers need a window

    Across the internet the peer link runs in the app window or a browser tab. A headless peer needs a native WebRTC part that is not built yet; on the same network, hivedesk invite --lan works from a terminal.

    No TURN relay

    Two very strict networks may not connect. hivedesk doctor --p2p tells which side is the problem, and both peers on one network can use the LAN.

    Not signed yet

    The downloads are not code signed or notarized. That is why macOS remote control is view only, and why your system warns before the first run.

    One model per lender

    A friend's machine serves only the model it has loaded. It never swaps the owner's model and never downloads one for a peer.

    Giant models are experimental

    Pooling one model across friends' machines over the encrypted p2p link is in 1.0, but off by default, and its speed depends on the network link between the machines.

    Small models are slow

    Workers take turns on one model, so a job takes minutes. On the CPU only, a model writes a few tokens per second. Big apps in one go are out of reach.

    FAQ

    Questions,
    answered plainly.

    Do I need an account or an API key?

    No. The default is a model that runs on your own machine, and friends connect to you directly. Keys are optional, only to use your own API key for a cloud model; they stay in a key store on your machine.

    Can a friend see my files?

    Only if you tick Read the workspace for them. When a friend joins, only See the City is on. Edits they propose wait for your approval, new files included.

    What does a friend's GPU see when I borrow it?

    The prompts and answers of the jobs sent to it, nothing else: no files, tools or memory. Its owner can stop any job, and any tool call that comes from its answer needs your approval.

    Do I need the desktop app?

    No. npx hivedesk runs the same engine and opens the window in your browser. The app adds the tray, system notifications and a folder picker, and keeps lending with the window closed. The table above lists every difference.

    Can someone take over my screen?

    Not without your click. Remote screen is off until you allow your address, every request needs your Accept, view only is the default, every grant expires, a locked screen takes no input, and one hotkey or the Stop button ends it all.

    Will it run on a laptop without a GPU?

    Yes, on the CPU with a small model, slowly: a few tokens per second. Small tasks work. A friend's GPU, with their permission, makes bigger models possible.

    What if two networks cannot connect?

    Run hivedesk doctor --p2p. It checks the relays, your ports and your network type and says which side blocks the link. There is no TURN relay built in; on one network, use the LAN option.

    Can I look around without downloading a model?

    Yes: npx hivedesk --demo plays a City in its own temporary folder, and every scripted event is labeled as a demo. It never touches your projects.

    How do I remove it?

    Delete ~/.hivedesk/ (your identity, known peers, the runtime, the models and your settings) and the .hivedesk/ folder in your project. If you installed it with npm, also run npm rm -g hivedesk.
